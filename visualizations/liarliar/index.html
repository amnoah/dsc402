Project 3: Liar Liar

For this assignment, I chose to work with political approval data for a U.S. president. I'll note that I have chosen to use data for a U.S. president who hasn't served in nearly 20 years to try to avoid creating anything that could be used for spreading misinformation - President George W. Bush.

How easy/hard is misleading people with visualizations?

It is so incredibly easy to create misleading displays, it was actually shocking. To create the display I did, I didn't have to alter the data or really mess with graph settings at all. It truly just took a small filter on the X-axis and Y-axis to abuse the data into showing whatever I wanted to. I'm sure there are much more complicated ways that this could be done as well that would be harder to figure out, but this is effective in my mind and incredibly simple.

How can readers avoid being misled?

Spend a little bit of time to read the graph and all of its different elements carefully. If the graph is showcasing certain years, question why those specific years are being used. If the Y-axis ends at certain numbers, try to imagine how it would look like with different ones and why those numbers may have been chosen. Lastly, verify what the data actually covers. My charts, for example, don't cover political approval - they cover Democratic political approval. Republican and Independent charts look way different, and this could tell a different story.

Charts

Genuine Graph

This graph is an honest representation of the approval rating of President George W. Bush by Democrats during his presidency. It represents both of his terms and generally has data polled every 2 weeks.

I tried to represent all data as honestly as I could. For example, the Y-axis data is percentages, so the graph shows all data points with the limits set to 0 and 100. The X-axis represents time during his presidency, so it has all collected data points within his presidency. Altogether, I tried to make it fair and honest.

Generally, it shows that his approval rating started somewhat low and it went even lower.

Misleading Graph

This graph is a misleading representation of the approval rating of President George W. Bush by Democrats during his presidency. It focuses specifically on post-9/11 and alters scales to try to make it appear like he experienced a significant decline.

While looking at the original data, I noticed that there was a huge spike in support following 9/11. I noticed that starting the graph here would make his approval decline appear much more significant than it would otherwise. As such, this graph starts at President Bush's approval peak after 9/11, and follows until his low at the end of his presidency. It also has the Y-axis scale adjusted to try to fit the line as perfectly within the chart as possible. This makes the changes look even more extreme than they are. This features cherrypicking and scale alterations.

It appears to show that his approval rating started extremely high and that it plummeted throughout his presidency.
